Kitchen and bar screens

A smart kitchen display system (KDS) instead of paper tickets. The kitchen ticket (KOT) and the bar ticket (BOT) both become screens. Each dish goes to the station that cooks it, slow dishes start first so a table's plates finish together, mains wait for the starters, and the pass shows what is ready to run.

The one-tap rules

  • Tap a dish when it is ready. All ready clears the whole ticket from the station.
  • Served ✓ on the pass closes a course. Fire next course now starts held mains early.
  • Recent brings back a ticket bumped by mistake.
  • A red box is an allergy or diet note. Read it aloud.
  • The server's tablet says if mains are being held. Send & fire all skips holding.

2 · Where the screens go

Touch screens that wipe clean, on the wall, off the cooking line.

Heat and grease. S1 hangs 0.36 m from the hood's end and about 1.5 m from the stock-pot burner. Use a sealed-front commercial screen (or a polycarbonate cover), a steel heat shield on the burner side, and keep it off the wok end of the line. If it cooks or fogs, move it 0.5 m west along the wall.
Network and power. Put the POS computer, the Wi-Fi router and a 5-port switch on a 1 kVA UPS at the counter, so a power cut does not stop orders. Run a network cable to S1 and S2 in the kitchen (steam and heat disturb Wi-Fi); S3 and the waiters' tablets can use Wi-Fi. Give the POS computer a fixed address on the router.

3 · How it decides

Station and time

Each dish has a station (where it is cooked), a usual cook time and a course. They are set from the menu below the first time the POS starts, and the owner can change any of them on the item (Menu, edit item).

Start order

Dishes of one course on one table are timed together. The slowest starts at once; the quicker ones show "start in 1:58" and light up when it is time, so everything finishes together.

Courses

On dine-in tables, mains wait until the starters are ready, plus a gap (8 minutes to begin with). Dessert waits for the mains. A held course always fires after 35 minutes. Takeaway and delivery are never held, and drinks are never held.

When it is late

Amber when a ticket passes the longest cook time on it; red three minutes after that. A cancelled dish appears as a red card, so nobody cooks it.

The current menu, routed

The POS's own menu list was loaded earlier (for example "Chimney soup") and does not match the menu above yet. Names it does not know are routed by their category and keywords, and you can set each one by hand.

5 · Setting it up

  1. Buy three touch screens (sizes in the table above) with a wall bracket each. Any Android, Windows or Chromebook all-in-one that runs Chrome works.
  2. Mount them. Run network cable to S1 and S2.
  3. On the counter computer, start the POS (Start POS.command). It prints the address of the computer, for example http://192.168.0.226:8080.
  4. In the POS, add a staff account for the kitchen (role: kitchen) under Staff. Log in on each screen with that PIN.
  5. Open one address on each screen, then choose "Add to Home Screen" and tap the screen once: /#/kds/hot for S1, /#/kds/pass for S2, /#/kds/bar for S3. Switch the screen's sleep off.
  6. Put a test order through: soup, a wok dish, a dim sum and a beer. The hot line should show three dishes with different start times and the bar should show the beer.
  7. Under Settings, then Printers, leave "Always print paper KOT and BOT" unticked. Keep the kitchen thermal printer (K18) as the backup, set to Network or System queue, and tick the offline fallback.
  8. Check every dish's station and time under Menu, edit item. Change anything the cooks disagree with.

7 · What is not verified

  • Cook times and courses are my estimates from the menu. Cooks should correct them in the first week; the screen then times everything from your numbers.
  • I tested the POS side on a copy with sample orders and looked at screenshots of the three screens. I have not run it on real screens, on your Wi-Fi, or with a real thermal printer.
  • Screen sizes are typical, plus or minus 0.02 m by brand. Pick a sealed commercial model for S1.
  • Mounting heights and viewing distances come from the 3D model's walls. Check them against the real cook's eye line on site.
  • The fallback paper ticket needs a Network or System printer. A Browser printer is not used, because it would open a print dialog on the waiter's tablet.
