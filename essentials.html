Small things people forget

  • One colour for one job. Red board, red bin and red cloth are for raw meat; green is vegetables; blue is seafood and glass; yellow is the bar and cooked food. Staff learn it in a day, and it prevents cross-contamination without a rule book.
  • Sharp knives are safe knives. Hone daily, stone weekly. A dull knife slips. Never leave a knife in the sink: wash it at once and put it on the strip.
  • Goa is humid. Boards, wooden spoons, brooms and mop heads must dry standing up in air; sack, flour and rice go into lidded bins; nothing lives on the floor.
  • Wet floors hurt people. Mats in front of the line and the sinks, non-slip shoes for everyone, a wet-floor sign before the mop comes out.
  • Power cuts. Keep a torch and an emergency lamp in the kitchen and the store room; finish the dish in the pan, then switch the gas off.
  • Chemicals live in the locked cupboard, labelled, never decanted into drink bottles; the cleaner signs the key in and out.
  • Order twice what you break. Colanders, brushes, scourers and gloves go every month: buy spares of the cheap things once, at the start.

Left out on purpose

Not must-haves, so not here: a planetary mixer, a food processor, a meat grinder, a mandoline, a vacuum sealer, a second set of woks, a stick blender, a bread knife and a dedicated cheese knife. Add them when sales prove the need. The equipment plan lists the big ones as "left out, add when".
