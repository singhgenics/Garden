KOT and BOT flow

One order on a tablet or phone becomes two tickets: the KOT for the kitchen and the BOT for the bar. This page shows who gets what, in what order, and how many copies are needed.

One order, two ticketsFood items form the KOT. Drinks form the BOT. The waiter never sorts them by hand.
Paper copies: 1 per stationThe old triplicate book needed 3 copies. A POS keeps the record itself, so one copy per station is enough.
With screens: 0 paperThe kitchen and bar screens replace the paper. A printer is only the backup.

The flow, step by step

Guest and waiter

1. Waiter takes the order

On the tablet or phone: pick the table, add dishes and drinks, add notes ("no peanuts", "less spicy"). Allergies go in the note.

tap Send KOT
POS server on the counter computer

2. The POS records and splits the order

It saves the order, gives it a ticket number, deducts stock, and sorts every item to its station: food to the kitchen, drinks to the bar.

Pass and waiter

5. Pass shows "Ready to run"

The waiter sees the table's food and drinks together and serves. Tapping Served closes the ticket.

Counter

6. Bill from the same order

GST invoice for food, VAT invoice for liquor, built from the order already in the system. No ticket is copied or re-keyed.

If a screen goes offline, the same KOT or BOT prints on that station's thermal printer instead.

How many copies you need

These are common restaurant practice, not a legal rule. Nothing in GST or the Goa VAT rules fixes the number of KOT copies. What the law cares about is the bill and its record.

Traditional paper book3 copies3 copiesOriginal to the kitchen or bar. Duplicate to the cashier, who bills from it. Triplicate stays with the waiter as proof of what was ordered.
POS with printers1 copy1 copyOne printout per station. The cashier and the waiter need no copy because the POS already holds the order.
POS with screens (Konso plan)0 paper0 paperThe ticket lives on the kitchen and bar screens. The printer is the backup when a screen goes offline.

One printed KOT per station is the sensible minimum even with a POS. If the pass wants its own paper copy to track tables, that is a second copy for the kitchen only, and the screens do the same job.

What happens when things change

Guest adds a dish later

The waiter taps Send KOT again. A new ticket, with the next number, goes only to the station that needs it. The earlier ticket is untouched.

Guest cancels a dish

If the kitchen already has it, a red CANCELLED card shows on that station's screen until someone taps OK. A manager PIN is needed to approve the void.

Table moves or merges

The POS supports moving and merging tables, so the order and its tickets stay together on one bill.

Item runs out (86)

Long-press the item and mark it 86 (out of stock), so waiters stop selling it.
