Crockery & glassware

Everything that goes on the table or across the bar, for 40 covers with two sets per cover. Every dish and drink on the current menu is matched to a piece below, and every piece is chosen to sit in the room: burgundy cloths, bottle-green walls, chocolate tile, brass and walnut.

The look

Six materials, used the same way everywhere, so the table looks planned and stays easy to restock. Light pieces carry the food so wok-browns, chilli-reds and greens show up under 2200 K light on a dark cloth; green and blue add the room's colours; brass ties in the rail, lamps and chopstick rests.

Ivory stoneware, chocolate rimThe base ware: plates, platters, wide bowls, coffee cups. Matches the chocolate tile; the warm white reads well on burgundy.

Bottle-green reactive glazeSoup, dessert and bone bowls: the wall colour on the table. Ivory inside so soups and ice cream still show.

Blue-and-white porcelainRice bowls, dip dishes, soup spoons, condiment pots: the cobalt of the pillars, the ginger jars and the bowl in the oil painting.

Brushed brass and goldChopstick rests, cutlery (gold PVD steel), the condiment caddy and bar tools. Repeats the brass rail and lamps.

Amber ribbed glassWater tumblers and carafes only. Glows under the orbs and hides water marks. Beer, whisky and cocktail glasses stay clear.

Walnut, bamboo and red lacquerBoards, lazy susan and paddles in dark walnut; bamboo steamers; a red lacquer bill box on every table.

One change from the decor renders. The renders show wine glasses at each place. There is no wine on the menu, so the place setting uses the amber water tumbler instead. The decor page also listed matte black cutlery; the renders show gold, and gold matches the brass better, so this list uses brushed gold. Pick one and buy the whole range from that one supplier.

How the numbers were worked out

  • On every place setting (×80): 40 covers × 2 sets. One set is on the tables while the second is in the three-bowl wash or drying. This covers plates, rice bowls, dips, soup spoons, chopsticks and rests, cutlery, napkins and water glasses.
  • Serving pieces and bar glasses: two sets as well, but of what a full house actually uses at once, not 80 of everything. Example: at a busy moment about 20 dim sum baskets are on tables or in the steamer, so you buy 40. Each card shows its own count line.
  • Paper and disposables: shown as a first month's stock at about 60 covers a day. Reorder from what you actually use.
  • Breakage: not included. After the first month, top up about 10% of the ceramics and glass from the same batch; glaze colour changes from batch to batch.

Bringing it to the table

  • Drinks go on the round tray, carried flat on the fingertips of the left hand, close to the body. Load the heaviest and tallest glasses in the middle, unload from the outside in. Hold glasses by the base or stem, never by the rim.
  • Two plates or fewer: carry by hand. Three or more, or anything with a claypot or steamer stack: food tray, carried at shoulder height, with the tray stand opened at the table first.
  • Hot pieces travel on their underplate. Soup bowls on a side plate, steamer baskets on a side plate, claypots on their wire stand; pick them up with the service cloth.
  • Serve from the guest's right, clear from the right, plates set down with the main item toward the guest. Shared dishes go in the middle of the table; rice bowls, dip dishes and soup spoons arrive with the dish that needs them.
  • Never put a tray on the guest table and never carry used plates and fresh food together. Clear on the way back, not on the way out.

Before you buy

  • Order one sample of each piece first and set one full table on a burgundy cloth under a warm bulb at night. Colours look different from the catalogue; check them in your real light before ordering 80.
  • You wash by hand (three-bowl sink, no dishwasher). Choose vitrified (fully fired) stoneware with a rolled or thick rim: it chips less when stacked in a sink. Avoid thin bone china and gold-painted rims; gold paint wears off.
  • Goa air is salty and humid. Ask for lacquered or PVD-coated brass so it does not go green, and never use bleach or scouring powder on the gold cutlery. Bamboo steamers and walnut boards must dry fully, standing open, or they go mouldy in the monsoon; keep 10% spare steamers.
  • Beer and bar glasses: buy tempered (toughened) glass with a fill line marked at 330 ml and 500 ml so pours are honest and checkable under weights-and-measures rules.
  • Buy every range from one supplier and one batch and note the product codes here, so breakage can be replaced with an exact match.
  • Size for the 80 × 80 cm tables: a 26 cm plate and a 45 cm lazy susan are the largest that fit four covers with shared dishes. Avoid 30 cm chargers.
