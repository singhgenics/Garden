Exhaust and air · notes
Drainage · notes
How the kitchen exhaust works. The hood K5 captures heat, steam and grease over the four cooking units. Grease filters catch the oil, a welded duct leaves through the dish-wash wall, crosses the dish wash and rises to a roof fan and cowl (X1 to X3), open to the sky above the dish-wash north wall, and the fan pulls about 5,000 to 6,000 m³ of air an hour out of the room (estimate). Air has to come back in: the make-up louvre on the west wall (X4), the open pass window and the door supply about 80% of it. Without it the kitchen goes under negative pressure, the flames flicker and doors slam.
How the drains work. The kitchen floor falls 1:100 to the hot-line trough (Y1). The trough (the north end of its east leg) drains through the north wall into the dish-wash drain together with the sink K28; the veg sink runs along the north wall to the trough and the hand basin under the floor to it; the two cell sinks and the floor grating feed the same line (Y4 to Y5). It leaves through the east wall into one grease trap outside (Y6), then an inspection chamber (Y7) and the outfall. Kitchen and dish-wash water pass through the grease trap; the washroom does not. The outfall point (sewer, septic tank or soak pit) is not known and must be confirmed on site.
