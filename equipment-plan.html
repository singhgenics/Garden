1 · Kitchen

5.41 × 4.38 m wall to wall · 5.21 × 4.18 m clear ·

Full schedule with notes
Refrigeration is one dual-zone upright plus one under-counter chiller. K6/K7 is a single 1.20 m cabinet with a chiller door (K6) and a freezer door (K7). Raw meat, seafood and cooked pork live on the K6 side; frozen dumplings, prawns, fries and ice cream on the K7 side. K8 holds vegetables, dairy, eggs and sauces. An under-counter freezer is not needed at the start: the stock plan puts about 130 L of frozen food into roughly 300 L of freezer. Add one when the K7 side is regularly full.
K13 hand-wash basin moved. It was in the south-west corner with the fridges directly in front of it. It is now on the south wall, between K8 and the store door, with about 1.4 m of open floor in front (the island is 1.48 m away) and a soap and towel dispenser above. The corner it left (0.45 m) is for the broom, dustpan and wet-floor sign.
Hot line. The four cooking units total 3.82 m on the east wall, under one hood. That leaves 0.36 m of the 4.18 m wall free. The island leaves 1.08 m of aisle on each side, and 1.36 m between the island and the cooks.
Storage is now in the store room. The kitchen keeps only working stock: U1 and U2 under the granite and U4 under the pass shelf; there are no wall cabinets. Bulk dry goods, packaging, drinks, spirits and chemicals are next door through the store door (0.9 m, beside the cooking line). The stock plan says what lives where and how it rotates.
Veg and non-veg are kept apart. Raw meat and seafood are prepared only on the island (K9), next to the K6 chiller side. Vegetables are prepared only at the west end of the granite platform (K10), beside the veg wash sink (K21). Raw meat never goes on K10. Plates are finished at the pass shelf (K11). K13 is the only basin for hands; never wash hands in K21.

No oven, grill, tandoor, dishwasher or mixer

Nothing on the menu is baked or grilled. Pork belly braises in a pot on the stock range, bao is steamed from frozen, and everything else is wok, fryer or steamer.

2 · Dish wash

5.41 × 2.00 m wall to wall · 5.21 × 1.80 m clear ·

Full schedule with notes
Hand wash in three bowls, no dishwasher. Dirty ware lands on D1, is scraped and pre-rinsed, goes into the wash bowl of D2, then the rinse bowl of D2, then the sanitise bowl D3, and air-dries on D4. D3 sits exactly where the hood dishwasher stood (0.85 m), so the run is unchanged at 5.17 m of the 5.21 m wall. Sanitiser strength is checked with test strips at every shift. A hood dishwasher can go back into this slot later.

The wall between the dish wash and the kitchen has a shuttered window over the granite platform (2.3 to 3.3 m from the west wall). A clean-ware hatch at the clean table (3.9 to 5.2 m) would save the walk round through the passage. That is a suggestion, not in the model.

3 · Bar

Back bar 2.80 m · counter 2.80 m · coffee station 1.30 m

Fix needed on the bar depth. In the 3D model the back bar is 0.42 m deep and the counter's working face is 1.00 m from the east wall, so the bartender aisle is only 0.58 m. A working bar needs about 0.90 m, and the under-counter machines are 0.60 m deep. This plan puts the counter's working face 1.50 m from the wall (0.50 m further into the hall): back bar 0.60 m, aisle 0.90 m, counter stand 0.60 m. Measure the real counter on site before ordering. Check that the move does not clash with the nearest tables.
Full schedule with notes
POS desk with a cash drawer. B17 stands at the north end of the bar, beside the coffee station, so the guest paying and the coffee order meet at one place. It is counter-height in the same royal-blue cabinet as the coffee station, with a marble-effect top for the terminal, receipt printer and card reader. The cash drawer B17a is fixed under the desk top (a lockable 41 × 42 cm POS drawer, with a banking tray and the float), and the router, switch and UPS sit in the cupboard below it. The desk is one till for the whole place; take the day's cash out at close and lock the drawer.
Leaner bar for the start. No ice machine, glass washer or shot freezer: ice comes in bags and lives in the B7 ice well, glasses are hand-washed, and chilled shots come from B9 and the ice. The bays for them are kept (B2 is a plain cabinet for now), so each can be added later without moving anything. The back-bar cabinet B4 (the old POS nook) now holds tea, coffee and filters.
Bar storage follows the store room. B1 and B9 hold about a day and a half of cold drinks, milk and citrus. Cases of beer, soft drinks and mixers, spare spirits, tea, coffee and bar paper are in the store room and come across at opening. B8 keeps about a week of sealed spirits; the rest stay in the locked cage. Spare kegs still have no cold place (see the stock plan).

If the counter cannot move, use a slimline bottle cooler that is no more than 0.42 m deep. Glasses are washed in the bar sink B7 and in the dish-wash bowls.

4 · Store room

2.70 × 6.46 m wall to wall · 2.50 × 6.26 m clear · door from the kitchen at the north end ·

Full schedule with notes
Laid out for rotation. Racks are 0.50 m deep on both sides of a 1.46 m aisle, so every shelf is reached from the aisle and nothing is stored behind anything. Fast-moving stock (oils, rice, drinks, vegetables) is nearest the door; slow stock (paper, spices, bar dry goods) is further in; spirits and chemicals are locked at the far end. Heavy tins, sacks and cases are on the bottom shelf, bottles and jars above. New stock goes in behind or beneath the old.
It has a lot of room. The six racks give roughly 6,000 L of shelf space against about 400 L a week of dry stock (my estimate from the stock plan), so you can buy dry goods fortnightly or monthly for a better price. The limit is shelf life and cash, not space.
Air, water and pests. The room has no window. A high louvre with an extractor fan on the east wall (X6) pulls air through a grille low in the store door, so onions and potatoes do not sweat and the room does not hold heat. There is no water or drain in the room; the chemical cupboard stands on a bund tray. Keep the store door closed to the kitchen steam, with a brush strip under it.
Not in the store room. Anything that needs a chiller or freezer, LPG cylinders (outside, open air), and spare kegs for more than 48 hours (no cold place yet). The old bunk room is gone: staff changing and lockers need a home elsewhere; I haven't planned one.

Exhaust and air · notes
Drainage · notes
How the kitchen exhaust works. The hood K5 captures heat, steam and grease over the four cooking units. Grease filters catch the oil, a welded duct rises through the roof to a fan and cowl (X1 to X3), and the fan pulls about 5,000 to 6,000 m³ of air an hour out of the room (estimate). Air has to come back in: the make-up louvre on the west wall (X4), the open pass window and the door supply about 80% of it. Without it the kitchen goes under negative pressure, the flames flicker and doors slam.
How the drains work. The kitchen floor falls 1:100 to the hot-line trough (Y1). The trough, the veg sink and the hand basin drain through the north wall into the dish-wash drain; the dish-wash sinks, mop sink and floor grating feed the same line (Y4 to Y5). It leaves through the east wall into one grease trap outside (Y6), then an inspection chamber (Y7) and the outfall. Kitchen and dish-wash water pass through the grease trap; the washroom does not. The outfall point (sewer, septic tank or soak pit) is not known and must be confirmed on site.
Savings from the leaner start. No dishwasher, ice machine or glass washer means no 3-phase booster heater, less steam, and three fewer drain and power connections. One upright instead of two means less heat and one less circuit. The big-ticket items left out are the dishwasher, the ice machine, the glass washer and the second upright; section 8 lists what to add and when.

9 · What is not verified

  • The kitchen depth (4.38 m), the dish-wash depth (2.00 m), the store-room size and the wall heights are estimates in the model. Measure them on site.
  • Equipment sizes are typical Indian market sizes, plus or minus 0.05 m by brand. Get the vendor's drawing before you order each unit.
  • Fridge and freezer capacities (about 300 L a side, about 250 to 280 L for K8) are typical figures, not a brand's. The fit is my estimate from the stock plan.
  • The hood airflow, duct and fan sizes, make-up air, store-room fan, grease-trap size, pipe sizes, electrical load and gas quantity are estimates. Have the HVAC vendor, a plumber, an electrician and the gas supplier confirm them.
  • Where the drains run and where the outfall goes (sewer, septic tank or soak pit) are not known. Floor falls and pipe routes are drawn as intent, not as a survey.
  • FSSAI, fire NOC, panchayat, drainage and LPG storage rules for Goa have not been checked. An advocate or consultant should confirm them.
  • The store door on the kitchen's south wall opens 3.47 to 4.37 m from the west wall, into the store room. Check its swing against the hand basin K13 and the hot-line end.
