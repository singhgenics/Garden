Start here

The 10 rules

Everyone follows these, every day, including the owners. If you remember nothing else, remember these.

  1. Say hello within 30 secondsSmile and say "Welcome to Cabin East." Even if your hands are full.
  2. Water and menus within 2 minutesBefore anything else.
  3. Always ask about allergiesEvery table. Write it on the order and tell the chef yourself.
  4. Everything goes through the tabletNo order is cooked or poured because someone shouted it. No ticket, no cooking.
  5. Wash your handsAfter the toilet, touching bins, your phone, raw meat or money. Soap, 20 seconds.
  6. Hot food hot, cold food coldWipe the plate rim. Never serve a chipped plate or a dish that looks wrong. Remake it.
  7. Phones in pocketsNot in your hand on the floor, at the bar or in the kitchen.
  8. Never argue with a guestListen, say sorry, fix it, call an owner. That is all.
  9. Drinks: 21 and over, and soberNo alcohol for anyone under 21. No more drinks for anyone who is drunk.
  10. Clean as you go, and say thank youWipe spills straight away. Say "Thank you, see you soon" to every guest at the door.

How one order goes

This is the whole journey, from a guest sitting down to the table being ready for the next guest. The coloured tag shows who does each step.

Watch it in 44 seconds (with sound), then read the steps.

  1. ServerGreet and seat30 seconds
    Walk to the guest, smile, show them a table.
  2. ServerWater and menus2 minutes
    Say the special. Ask "any allergies?"
  3. ServerTake the order on the tabletby 4 minutes
    Take drinks and food in the same visit. Read the order back to the guest.
  4. TabletTap SENDby 5 minutes
    One tap. The food appears on the kitchen screen and the drinks on the bar screen. Mains wait until the starters are ready. Check that the tablet says "Sent".
  5. BartenderMake the drinks5 minutes
    Make them in the order they show on the bar screen, oldest first. Tap each ticket when it is done. Put the drinks on the bar counter with the table number. Ring the bell.
  6. Chef + helperCook the food15 minutes
    Chef reads the hot-line screen aloud. A dish that says "start in 1:58" waits; when it lights up green, start it. This way every plate finishes together. Tap each dish when it is ready. Check the plate. Ring the bell.
  7. ServerServewithin 90 seconds of the bell
    Drinks first, then all the food together. Say what each dish is.
  8. ServerCheck back, then the bill2 minutes after serving
    "Is everything good?" Offer another drink. Print the bill when they ask. Take payment. Say thank you.
  9. CleanerReset the table3 minutes
    Clear, wipe, reset cutlery, napkins, sauces and menus. The table is ready again.

The ticket system

The kitchen and bar screens

There are three screens: the hot line screen in the kitchen, the bar screen at the back bar, and the pass screen by the pass window. When the server taps Send on the tablet, the food shows up on the kitchen screen and the drinks on the bar screen by themselves. There is no paper. This is how nobody forgets anything and nobody has to shout.

  • When a new ticket appears the screen beeps. Say "Heard" out loud so the server knows it arrived.
  • Cook and pour in the order the tickets came, oldest first. A ticket turns amber when it is late and red when it is very late. A red box is an allergy or diet note: read it aloud.
  • A dish that is dimmed and says "start in 1:58" is waiting for a slower dish. Start it when it lights up.
  • Tap a dish when it is ready, or All ready for the whole ticket. The pass screen then says READY TO RUN for that table. The server taps Served after taking it out.
  • Mains are held until the starters are ready (plus a few minutes). If the guests want the mains now, tap Fire next course now on the pass screen.
  • Want to add a dish later? Tap Send again. Never tell the chef by mouth only.
  • Wrong item? Ask an owner to cancel it on the tablet. A red CANCELLED card shows on the screen: tap OK when you have seen it.
  • Screen not working? After about a minute the POS prints the ticket on the backup printer by itself. If the whole system is down, write the order on the pad (table, items, time), take it to the kitchen, and type it in later.
  • The chef tells the servers when a dish has run out. Tell the guest before they order it.
Who does what

Each person's job

One card each. Do what your card says and you are doing the job properly.

Servers (the two owners)

Look after the guests and the money.

Split the room

  • Server 1: the tables and seats near the bar.
  • Server 2: the tables near the entrance, the kitchen pass and the bill.
  • If any guest has waited 30 seconds, whoever is nearest says hello. No table belongs to only one person.

Before opening

  • Clean clothes, wash hands, phone in pocket.
  • Tablet charged and logged in.
  • Check every table: cutlery, chopsticks, napkins, sauces, clean menu.
  • Ask the chef what has run out and what today's special is.

During service

  • Follow the 9 steps in "How one order goes".
  • Walk past each table every few minutes. Offer another drink when a glass is nearly empty.
  • Clear finished plates quickly.

Never

  • Never leave a table waiting without a word.
  • Never serve alcohol to someone under 21 or someone who is drunk.
"Welcome to Cabin East. Please sit anywhere you like. I'll bring water and menus right now."

Bartender

Draught beer, spirits, coffee and soft drinks.

Before opening

  • Check the 3 beer kegs and the gas are connected. Pour a little and throw it away.
  • Ice full, lemons and garnish cut, glasses clean and dry.
  • Check the bar screen is on and says nothing is waiting.
  • Tell an owner if anything is running low.

During service

  • Make drinks in the order they show on the bar screen. Say "Heard".
  • Beer: clean glass, tilt it, pour so it ends with about 2 cm of foam.
  • Measure every spirit with the jigger (the small measuring cup). The system counts stock by what you pour.
  • Put drinks on the bar with the table number and ring the bell.
  • Wipe the counter as you go.

Never

  • No alcohol for anyone under 21. If someone looks young, ask for ID.
  • Stop serving anyone who is drunk and call an owner.
  • Do not leave the bar empty.

After closing

  • Clean the beer taps, switch off the beer, wipe the bar, empty the ice, wash glasses, mop.

Chef

Responsible for every plate that leaves the kitchen.

Before opening

  • Gas on at the main valve. No smell of gas. Extinguisher in place.
  • Check the fridge is cold and the freezer is frozen. Write it on the sheet.
  • Prepare sauces and cut ingredients so cooking is fast. Label every container with the name and date.
  • Tell the servers what has run out and what the special is.

During service

  • Read each slip aloud. Cook oldest slip first.
  • Start the dim sum first (it is the slowest), then the soup, then the wok.
  • Allergy slip: clean board, clean tools, clean wok. Hand the plate to the server yourself.
  • Before it leaves: right dish, hot, rim wiped. Ring the bell once.
  • Cook chicken and pork all the way through. No pink.

After closing

  • Cover and label all leftover food. Use older food first next day.
  • Gas off at the main valve. Clean the stove and the floor.

Kitchen helper

The chef's helping hands.

Before opening

  • Wash hands, clean apron, hair tied back.
  • Cut and portion what the chef tells you. Label everything.
  • Fill and heat the steamer.

During service

  • Say "Heard" when a slip prints. Put the dim sum in the steamer straight away.
  • Keep sauces, rice and plates topped up.
  • Wipe the plate rim before the chef checks it.
  • Wipe your work area every 15 minutes.

Never

  • Never use the same board or knife for raw meat and cooked food.

After closing

  • Empty and dry the steamer. Wash boards and knives. Write tomorrow's prep list for the chef.

Cleaner

Guests judge a place by its toilet and its tables.

Before opening

  • Mop the floor, wipe all tables, straighten the chairs, sweep the entrance and path.
  • Clean the toilet: soap, paper, empty bin, dry floor.
  • Set up the dish wash room so it is ready.

During service

  • Check the toilet every 30 minutes. Write the time and your initials on the sheet.
  • Take finished plates to the dish wash room quickly.
  • Reset each table within 3 minutes (see the list below).
  • Wipe any spill straight away.
  • Empty bins before they overflow.

Table reset: 5 things

  • Wipe and dry the table.
  • Straighten the chairs.
  • Cutlery, chopsticks and napkin for each seat.
  • Sauce set and menu, clean.
  • Look from the guest's chair. Would you sit there?

After closing

  • Mop everything, wash and empty all bins, sweep outside.

Don't panic

If it goes wrong

Every restaurant has bad moments. A guest remembers how you fixed it. Always: listen, say sorry once, fix it, tell an owner.

The food is late

After 12 minutes, check the kitchen. Tell the guest honestly: "Two more minutes, I'm sorry." Bring water. After 20 minutes an owner goes to the table.

Wrong or cold dish

Say sorry, take it away at once, ask the kitchen to remake it. Do not argue about whose mistake it was.

Allergy worry

Do not serve the dish. The chef remakes it with clean tools. If someone feels unwell, call an ambulance (108) and an owner.

An unhappy guest

Let them finish speaking. Do not defend. Say sorry and fix what you can. Anything bigger: fetch an owner.

A drunk guest

Stop serving alcohol. Offer water and food. An owner talks to them calmly. Never touch a guest.

Very busy

Do not rush and make mistakes. Tell new guests the real wait time ("about 20 minutes"). The helper only plates. The cleaner only washes dishes.

Power cut

Use a torch or phone light at the bar and kitchen. Keep the fridge shut. Roll the curtains up for air. Use the order pad if the system is off.

Fire or gas smell

Turn the gas off at the main valve. Use the fire blanket or extinguisher for a small fire. Walk guests out through the entrance. Call 101.

Before opening day

Practise first

The times on this page are good targets, not proven numbers. Test them before real guests arrive.

  1. Invite friends and family for 3 practice evenings. Fill every table.
  2. One person holds a stopwatch and writes down: time to greet, time to send, time for drinks, time for first dish, time for the last dish.
  3. Also time how long it takes to carry food from the kitchen to the farthest table.
  4. If the kitchen cannot manage 15 minutes when the hall is full, change the menu or tell guests a longer wait. Do not pretend.
  5. After opening, the tablet shows how long each order took. Check it each week.
